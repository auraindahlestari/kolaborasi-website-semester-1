How an Old Power Supply Casing Is Transformed into a Bluetooth Sound System

Open

Paragraph 1 - General Statement

Electronic waste is one of the environmental problems caused by the rapid development of technology. The metal casing of old computer power supply units (PSU) is often discarded when the internal components are damaged or no longer needed. One creative way to manage this metal waste is by transforming an old PSU casing into a trendy Bluetooth sound system enclosure. This transformation shows how rigid electronic waste can become a useful, aesthetically valuable, and economically beneficial product.

Paragraph 2 - The Transformation Process

The transformation begins when an unused power supply unit is disassembled to separate the outer iron casing from the internal electronic components. The metal casing is then thoroughly cleaned to remove dust, dirt, or remaining rust. At this stage, the sharp iron corners are smoothed down or covered to ensure safety during handling. Next, measurements and perforations are made on the casing to create mounting slots for new audio components, such as volume knobs, speaker holes, and power sockets.

Paragraph 3 - The Formation of a New Product

The prepared iron casing is then combined with modern audio components to create a new object. A Bluetooth amplifier module kit (such as the TPA3116D2) is placed inside the casing along with two 3-4 inch passive speaker units. Since the casing's original metal material can block wireless signals, the antenna or Bluetooth module is strategically positioned or slightly extended outside the casing to maintain a stable signal connection. All of these components are neatly wired and soldered together using tin wire, then connected to a 12V power adapter as the energy source.

Paragraph 4 - The Result and Its Impact

As a result, an old computer PSU casing is transformed into a unique Bluetooth sound system that has both functional and aesthetic value, showcasing a masculine and industrial design. The use of an iron casing provides a sturdy structure, where its solid weight effectively minimizes sound vibration (rattle) when playing music with heavy bass. The product can be used as a trendy household decoration while also reducing the accumulation of metal waste in landfills. Therefore, recycling computer components into audio devices is one way to support a more sustainable environment.

How an Old CPU Casing Is Transformed into an Automatic Egg Incubator

Open

Paragraph 1 - General Statement

Electronic waste is one of the environmental problems caused by the rapid development of technology. The metal casing of old computers is often discarded or left unused when the internal components become obsolete. One creative way to manage this electronic waste is by transforming an old CPU casing into a functional automatic egg incubator. This transformation shows how an discarded computer component can be repurposed into a highly useful tool with high educational and functional value, turning waste into a valuable asset for home-scale farming.

Paragraph 2 - The Transformation Process

The transformation begins by clearing out all the internal computer components from the unused CPU casing to create an empty, usable space. The casing is then thoroughly cleaned. To ensure proper temperature stability, excess air holes on the casing are sealed using cardboard or styrofoam. Since the thin metal walls of a CPU casing can easily lose heat, the entire interior structure is lined with thick styrofoam or cardboard as insulation, preventing the inside temperature from dropping during cold weather.

Paragraph 3 - The Formation of a New Product

The insulated casing is then equipped with electrical and mechanical components to regulate the internal environment. A digital thermostat module (XH-W3001) is installed on the outside of the casing for easy monitoring. Inside, two 5-watt incandescent lamps are fitted to act as the primary heat source, wired directly through the thermostat relay. An internal DC CPU fan is mounted to distribute and equalize the heat circulation evenly. Finally, a small water tray is placed at the base to maintain the required humidity, topped with a wire mesh layer that serves as the tray to safely hold the eggs.

Paragraph 4 - The Result and Its Impact

As a result, an old CPU casing is successfully transformed into a compact automatic egg incubator that is ideal for hobbyists or home-scale poultry farmers, capable of hatching 10 to 20 eggs at once. The system automatically maintains an optimal temperature of around 37.5°C to 38.5°C, ensuring a high hatching success rate. This product not only offers a practical and budget-friendly solution for small poultry breeders but also significantly reduces the accumulation of metal and plastic electronic waste. Therefore, recycling computer casings into agricultural tools is an effective way to support a more sustainable environment.

How Old CD Casings Are Transformed into a Mini Hanging Wall Shelf

Open

Paragraph 1 - General Statement

Plastic waste is one of the major environmental problems caused by the massive production of consumer goods. Old plastic compact disc (CD) casings are often thrown away or left gathering dust once digital media takes over. One creative way to manage this plastic waste is by transforming used CD casings into a minimalist mini hanging wall shelf. This transformation shows how fragile and discarded plastic items can be repurposed into a practical product with a modern Scandinavian aesthetic, providing a smart storage solution for lightweight household items.

Paragraph 2 - The Transformation Process

The transformation begins by preparing around 8 to 10 thick, hard plastic CD jewel cases and removing any clear covers from their bases. Any leftover paper inserts or stickers inside the cases are thoroughly cleaned and peeled off. Next, the clean cases are arranged to form 90-degree right angles, which will serve as the support structures for the shelves. Super glue (cyanoacrylate) is carefully applied along the hinges and joints to lock the cases firmly in place, ensuring the initial frame becomes rigid and stable.

Paragraph 3 - The Formation of a New Product

The individual L-shaped structures are then stacked vertically and glued together using strong adhesive to form a multi-tiered rack. To prepare the rack for hanging, a heated soldering iron is used to melt precise holes on the top-right and top-left edges of the frame. A piece of sturdy hemp rope or cord is threaded through these holes and tied with secure knots at the bottom to act as a weight-bearing support. Optionally, the entire plastic structure can be sprayed with paint, or left transparent to preserve its clean, modern look before it is mounted on the wall.

Paragraph 4 - The Result and Its Impact

As a result, old plastic CD cases are successfully transformed into a unique mini hanging wall shelf that has both functional and aesthetic value. The transparency of the plastic material gives a clean and spacious feel to the room, making it perfect for displaying small succulents, perfumes, or cosmetics. While it is lightweight and easy to install, users must avoid overloading it with heavy books or large glass bottles due to the brittle nature of plastic. Ultimately, recycling plastic cases into decorative organizers is an effective step toward reducing plastic waste and promoting a more sustainable lifestyle.
